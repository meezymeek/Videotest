You found NOMAD-X

A computer, a library, a radio room, and a power plant — in one case.

It works with no internet, no cell service, and no wall power. It makes its own Wi-Fi, has an AI assistant named NOMAD that lives inside, and has several ways to reach the outside world when nothing else does.

⏱If you have 60 seconds

  1. Keep the lid open while it runs. Keep it in shade.
  2. Wait for the dashboard (about 2 minutes after the MAIN switch). You're looking at it.
  3. Tap the chat box and type Hello, what can you do?
  4. To get help: type emergency and what's wrong. NOMAD sends it on every link.
  5. To turn off: type shut down, wait for black screen, flip MAIN off.
What is this thing?

Four things fused together

Tying them together is NOMAD, an assistant that runs on the computer in this case — not in the cloud. It can see the battery, hear the radios, send messages, look things up, and manage power.

🧠A good laptop + a small server

A compact AI computer ("the brain"). Runs the assistant, stores everything, serves the Wi-Fi apps.

📚A library

Offline Wikipedia with pictures, medical references, repair guides, maps, and field manuals — about 300 GB. Answers when there's no Google.

📡A radio shack + phone + satellite messenger

Ham radio, family radios, long-range mesh text radios, a cell modem, and a satellite text modem. Many ways out, so one always works.

🔋A solar generator

A big lithium battery, solar input, and software that stretches it. Days of use with no wall power.

What it's for

  • Off-grid camps and expeditions
  • Disasters and outages — relaying messages for a group
  • Remote work sites — network, files, docs, comms
  • A mobile radio and communications post
  • Tinkering: software radios, Wi-Fi survey, a shared GPU

What it is not

  • Not a phone, and not internet unless a Starlink dish or a cell tower is around
  • Not waterproof while running — it's a base station: open to run, closed to move
  • Not a cloud service: nothing you say leaves the case unless you ask it to send a message

How you talk to it

Plain English, from wherever you are

"How much battery is left?" · "Send Sam a message that we're moving camp" · "What's the weather on the radio?" · "How do I treat a burn?" — all work.

What NOMAD can do

  • Report the state of everything: battery, solar, links, temperature, location
  • Send messages on any link: mesh, text, satellite, radio, email-over-radio
  • Listen to radio channels and tell you what it heard
  • Scan the airwaves: weather radio, repeaters, aircraft nearby
  • Answer from the offline library and say where the answer came from
  • Manage power and warn you before it runs out
  • Run a self-test of every link: /comms-check
  • Keep a log of everything it did

What it won't do

  • Transmit on the ham radio unless a licensed operator asks — and only on allowed frequencies
  • Pretend. If a link is down or it doesn't know, it says so
  • Let the battery die: below 30% it saves power and tells you; below 15% it switches off non-essentials itself
  • Use the security tools on its own

Routines & safety

Setting up, daily, moving

Set up at a new site · 10 minutes

Tick as you go. This list is saved on this screen.

Daily

  • Morning: read NOMAD's 7 am brief
  • Keep the panel pointed at the sun; glance at the battery number
  • Night: ask "Will we make it to sunrise on battery?" and follow its advice

Moving

  • Type shut down, wait for black screen and silent fans, MAIN off
  • Unplug antennas and solar, cap the jacks, stow the Wi-Fi puck and keyboard, latch
  • Never travel with it running. Roll it on its wheels; stand it on edge only to operate

Safety & legal

  • Heat: lid open, in shade. The brain likes 5–30 °C (41–86 °F).
  • Water: closed and off it's weatherproof; open and running it isn't. Rain → shut down and close.
  • Electricity: don't open the power panel or bypass fuses.
  • Licenses (US): ham radio TX needs an FCC amateur license; GMRS handhelds need a GMRS license (no test). Mesh, satellite, cell, Wi-Fi: none. Listening is always legal. In a real emergency, rules allow any means to get help — use emergency.
  • Security tools: only on networks you own or have written permission to test.
